\section{Introduction and context}
\label{sec:intro}

This chapter states the objective, the platform, and the scope. It closes with the contributions and a reading map.

\subsection{The platform and the objective}
\label{sec:intro-objective}

DASH-01 is a bipedal robot built at REHAssist. It carries two legs, six quasi-direct-drive
actuators, a parallel 4-bar knee, a passive ankle spring and a single-board computer. The hardware
budget was roughly CHF\,5000. \Cref{fig:robot} shows the machine as modelled.

The objective at kickoff was maximum running speed, with the bipedal 100\,m record as the ambition. 
That record has moved several times during the project. It now stands at 8.86\,s, set by Tiangong Ultra in Beijing on August 25, 2026~\cite{apnews2026chinese}. That is an average of about 40.6\,km/h, faster than Usain Bolt's 9.58\,s.
Before this thesis started, the record was Cassie's 24.73\,s over the same distance, set in 2022~\cite{crowley2024cassie}.

The project inherited a partially finished robot. The work reported here therefore
starts at modelling, and at the selection of the non-mechanical subsystems. It then runs through
characterisation and the training of a policy. 


\subsection{The objective moved from record pace to a measured demonstrator}
\label{sec:intro-reframe}

\textbf{The record moved faster than the thesis.} New bipedal speed marks now appear at roughly a
monthly cadence, from teams with institutional budgets. 

\textbf{The measured plant does not admit record pace.} \Cref{sec:speed-ceiling} bounds this
mechanism at about 6\,m/s. The bound comes from first principles, by using the workspace of the leg and the impulse it can deliver. 

The objective of this thesis was therefore restated as a measured demonstrator plus a reusable method. The
deliverable is the chain from CAD to a trained policy. 

\textbf{The problem this addresses.} Reinforcement learning has produced legged-locomotion results
on research platforms. Reproducing them on a new, custom, imperfectly known robot is non-trivial. The plant parameters are uncertain. The actuators hide undocumented internal control
loops. The sensors are commodity parts. The gap between ``reinforcement learning walks in simulation'' and ``reinforcement learning walks on this robot'' is
dominated by modelling fidelity, actuator bandwidth and calibration.

\textbf{Sensing scope.} Proprioception and one base inertial measurement unit are the only sensors.
Foot impact sensors were dropped due to lack of time rather than on evidence. The hardware provision for them
remains in place, as four analogue inputs on the sensor hat. The literature prior that foot feedback
helps convergence is therefore untested here.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contrib}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{A CAD-to-MJCF pipeline}, with the loop-closure treatment URDF cannot express. The model was then validated against the
        backdriven workspace (\cref{sec:twin}).
  \item \textbf{A measured plant} (\cref{sec:plant-id}). It covers weighed segment masses, a torque
        constant identified by a known-mass method that breaks the degeneracy between $K_t$ and
        gravity, the Bode response of the drive's internal position loop, inertial-sensor noise
        characteristics, loop timing and the CAN protocol.
  \item \textbf{A first-principles capability analysis} of running speed on that plant, validated
        against MuJoCo (\cref{sec:speed-ceiling}).
  \item \textbf{An action-space and curriculum design} for the learned controller. 
        Multiple action spaces were tested, and a curriculum was built to converge to a running policy on the measured plant (\cref{sec:rl-cpg,sec:rl-curriculum}).
  \item \textbf{A deployment stack on the robot.} It is a 200\,Hz daemon on stock Linux, a web
        bring-up instrument that was used to perform the measurement of the robot characteristics.
        The deployment stack also includes safety mechanisms to prevent the robot from exiting the safe operating envelope (\cref{sec:deploy}).
  \item \textbf{Two component databases}, covering 140 commercial actuators and roughly 430 battery cells.
        They were used to validate the choice of the actuators and the battery pack (\cref{sec:hw-selection}, \cref{sec:app-db}).
\end{itemize}
