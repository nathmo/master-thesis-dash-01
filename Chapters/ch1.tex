\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Problem statement}

Reinforcement learning has produced legged-locomotion results on research platforms.
Reproducing those results on a new, custom, imperfectly known robot is a different problem, and it is the problem most robot builders actually face.
On such a robot the plant parameters are uncertain, the actuators hide undocumented internal
control loops, the sensors are commodity parts, and the computer is a single board. The gap
between ``RL walks in simulation'' and ``RL walks on this robot'' is dominated by modelling
fidelity, actuation bandwidth, calibration and instrumentation, not by learning algorithms.

DASH-01 is a two-legged, six-motor robot with a parallel-linkage knee and a passive ankle,
designed around the ambition of high running speed. This thesis takes the robot from a partially
finished mechanical design to a measured, simulated, trained and instrumented platform. It
reports what each stage cost and what each stage revealed, and it includes the negative results
deliberately.

\subsection{Objectives of the Project}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Model the robot faithfully enough to train against: kinematics, including the closed
        4-bar loop, plus masses, actuators and sensors.
  \item Establish the robot's physical performance envelope independently of any controller, so
        that learned results can be judged against a ceiling rather than against hope.
  \item Train locomotion policies with reinforcement learning. Iterate on the action-space design
        until one design converges reliably, then quantify what still separates those policies
        from hardware deployment.
  \item Build the deployment stack on the real robot (control loop, calibration, safety and
        telemetry), and validate as much of the chain as the hardware state allows.
  \item Record the methodology honestly enough to be reproducible, including the failures.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Methodology Overview}

The project starts halfway. The mechanical design existed in part, so the work reported here
begins at modelling and at hardware selection for the non-mechanical subsystems. The development
order was as follows. First, CAD re-assembly and export to a simulable model. Second, validation
of that model against the physical robot, by workspace calibration, then statics, then full
dynamic identification. Third, a first-principles capability analysis on the validated model.
Fourth, a toy problem (a single-joint inverted pendulum) to exercise the complete
train--export--deploy chain at minimum stakes. Fifth, three generations of RL policy design,
trained on an HPC cluster. Last, the deployment stack on the robot, driven by a measurement
campaign that fed corrections back into the simulation. \Cref{sec:overall-method} details the
sequence. The single most consequential methodological finding is that \emph{every} assumed plant
parameter that was later measured moved in the pessimistic direction (\cref{tab:plant-id}).

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A complete CAD-to-MJCF modelling pipeline for a biped whose linkage closes a loop. It
        includes the loop-closure treatment that URDF cannot express, and validation tooling:
        analytic Jacobians checked against finite differences, and loop closure checked against
        the measured backdriven workspace at 100\,\% of 2789 poses.
  \item A measured plant. It comprises weighed segment masses, torque constants identified by a
        known-mass method that breaks the degeneracy between $K_t$ and gravity, the Bode response
        of the drive's internal position loop (0.8\,Hz plus 25\,ms), IMU noise with provenance
        labels, loop timing and the CAN protocol. It states explicitly what the identification
        could \emph{not} resolve.
  \item A first-principles capability analysis of running speed, validated against MuJoCo. Its
        sensitivity result (distal mass costs roughly nine times what torso mass costs) was
        confirmed repeatedly by later measurement as the binding design axis.
  \item A policy architecture and the curriculum that trains it. The architecture is a learned
        Fourier gait generator, re-parameterised every step and mirrored across legs, with
        hard-wired reflexes, learned reflexes and a per-step residual channel. The curriculum
        releases the base degrees of freedom one at a time. An A/B against an explicit-oscillator
        generator ran on identical plants and rewards.
  \item A catalogue of failure modes with measured diagnoses. It covers reward exploits (skating,
        floor penetration, shuttling and suicide by penalty), curriculum deadlocks, evaluation
        restore bugs, a warm-start bug that silently duplicated every seed replicate, and the
        pitch-balance wall traced to plant statics rather than to learning.
  \item A scripted classical baseline: a capture point and a clocked gait, 21 gains, no learning.
        It outperforms RL on the constrained plant and fails identically on the free plant, which
        is evidence that the wall is physical.
  \item Deployment infrastructure on the robot: a 200\,Hz daemon on stock Linux, a web bring-up
        instrument that carried most of the measurement campaign, and a three-tier flight recorder
        with a pre-move safety guard. The recorder was built in response to a hardware failure and
        validated by reproducing that failure.
  \item Two published, reusable component databases, each with figure-of-merit tooling. The
        actuator explorer holds 140 commercial actuators, 121 motors, 121 gearboxes and 9 drives
        across eight vendors, and is built on the winding-invariant motor constant
        $K_m = K_t/\sqrt{R}$ with explicit electrical reference frames. The cell and pack explorer
        holds roughly 430 cells, 54 BMS modules and 110 packs, and is built on duration-stamped
        current tiers and a pack synthesiser. Both audited this robot's own hardware choices after
        the fact (\cref{sec:hw-motor-critique}). The audit vindicates the actuator selection as
        the Pareto-optimal choice in its class. It also shows that the battery was selected
        against a peak current rating quoted without a duration.
\end{itemize}
