\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Problem statement}

Reinforcement learning has produced striking legged-locomotion results on a small set of
well-instrumented research platforms. Reproducing those results on a new, custom, imperfectly
known robot is a different problem, and it is the problem most robot builders actually face: the
plant parameters are uncertain, the actuators have undocumented internal control loops, the
sensors are commodity parts, and the compute is a single-board computer. The gap between ``RL
walks in simulation'' and ``RL walks on this machine'' is dominated not by learning algorithms but
by modelling fidelity, actuation bandwidth, calibration, and instrumentation.

DASH-01 is a two-legged, six-motor robot with a parallel-linkage knee and a passive ankle,
designed around the ambition of high running speed. This thesis takes it from a partially
finished mechanical design to a measured, simulated, learning-trained and hardware-instrumented
platform, and reports — deliberately including the negative results — what each stage cost and
what it revealed.

\subsection{Objectives of the Project}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Model the robot faithfully enough to train against: kinematics (including the closed
        4-bar loop), masses, actuators, sensors.
  \item Establish the robot's physical performance envelope independently of any controller, so
        learned results can be judged against a ceiling rather than against hope.
  \item Train locomotion policies with reinforcement learning, iterating on the action-space
        design until one converges reliably, and quantify what remains between those policies and
        hardware deployment.
  \item Build the deployment stack — control loop, calibration, safety, telemetry — on the real
        robot, and validate as much of the chain as the hardware state allows.
  \item Record the methodology honestly enough to be reproducible, including the failures.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Methodology Overview}

The project is a hybrid start: the mechanical design existed in part, and the work here begins at
modelling and hardware selection for the non-mechanical subsystems. The development order was:
CAD re-assembly and export to a simulable model; validation of the model against the physical
robot (workspace, statics, then full dynamic identification); a first-principles capability
analysis on the validated model; a toy problem (a single-joint inverted pendulum) to exercise the
complete train--export--deploy chain at minimum stakes; three generations of RL policy design
trained on an HPC cluster; and finally the deployment stack on the robot, driven by a measurement
campaign that fed corrections back into the simulation. \Cref{sec:overall-method} details the
sequence; the single most consequential methodological finding is that \emph{every} assumed plant
parameter that was later measured moved in the pessimistic direction
(\cref{tab:plant-id}).

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A complete CAD-to-MJCF modelling pipeline for a closed-loop-linkage biped, including the
        loop-closure treatment URDF cannot express, and validation tooling (analytic Jacobians
        checked against finite differences, loop closure checked against the measured backdriven
        workspace at 100\,\% of 2789 poses).
  \item A measured plant: weighed segment masses, identified torque constants via a known-mass
        method that breaks the $K_t$/gravity degeneracy, the drive's internal position-loop Bode
        (0.8\,Hz + 25\,ms), IMU noise with provenance labels, loop timing, and CAN protocol —
        together with an explicit statement of what the identification could \emph{not} resolve.
  \item A first-principles running-speed capability analysis validated against MuJoCo, with a
        mass-location sensitivity result (distal mass $\sim$9$\times$ costlier than torso mass)
        that later measurements repeatedly confirmed as the binding design axis.
  \item A policy architecture — learned Fourier gait generator re-parameterised per step, mirrored
        across legs, plus hard-wired and learned reflexes and a per-step residual channel — and a
        base-DOF-release curriculum that trains it, with an A/B against an explicit-oscillator CPG
        generator on identical plants and rewards.
  \item A catalogue of failure modes with measured diagnoses: reward exploits (skating, floor
        penetration, shuttling, suicide-by-penalty), curriculum deadlocks, evaluation-restore
        bugs, a warm-start seed bug that silently duplicated every seed replicate, and the
        pitch-balance wall traced to plant statics rather than learning.
  \item A scripted classical baseline (capture-point + clocked gait, 21 gains, no learning) that
        outperforms RL on the constrained plant and fails identically on the free plant —
        evidence that the wall is physical.
  \item Deployment infrastructure on the robot: a 200\,Hz daemon on stock Linux, a web bring-up
        instrument through which most of the measurement campaign was performed, and a three-tier
        flight recorder with a pre-move safety guard, built in response to a hardware failure and
        validated by reproducing that failure.

\end{itemize}
