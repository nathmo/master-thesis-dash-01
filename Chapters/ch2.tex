\section{Background and State of the Art}

\subsection{Dynamic biped locomotion}

Running differs from walking in that the centre of mass follows a spring-like, not vaulting,
trajectory, with flight phases and ground contacts of tens of milliseconds. The reduced models
that organise the field — the inverted pendulum for walking, the spring-loaded inverted pendulum
(SLIP) for running, and capture-point/instantaneous-capture-point analysis for push recovery —
appear throughout this thesis in load-bearing roles: the capture point underlies both the
hand-wired pitch reflex (\cref{sec:rl-reflexes}) and the scripted baseline
(\cref{sec:scripted-baseline}), and the free-topple time of the inverted pendulum turns out to
\emph{be} the m3 failure signature (\cref{sec:scripted-baseline}). Stabilisation is hard because
the dynamics are underactuated during flight and contact, the useful control inputs (foot
placement, CoP modulation) are discrete or saturating, and the timescales are short relative to
actuator bandwidth — the last of which this project measured the hard way
(\cref{sec:plant-id-drive}).

\subsection{Control approaches}

Classical stacks decompose the problem: a gait/footstep planner, a whole-body or centroidal
controller, and joint-level tracking. They are transparent and analysable but demand accurate
models and high-bandwidth torque control. Learned controllers collapse the stack into a policy
trained in simulation, trading analysability for robustness to the messiness of contact — at the
price of a sim-to-real gap that must be closed with modelling fidelity and domain randomisation.
This thesis ends up using both: RL for the gait, and classical structure (reflexes, a clocked
generator, capture-point priors) exactly where feedback must not be blind
(\cref{sec:rl-reflexes}). The scripted baseline of \cref{sec:scripted-baseline} is the classical
counterfactual, built on the same plant to answer ``was the learning ever the bottleneck?''

\subsection{RL for locomotion}

The relevant lineage for this work is the family of \emph{periodically parameterised} action
spaces: Policies-Modulating-Trajectory-Generators (PMTG)~\cite{pmtg2018}, oscillator-based
CPG-RL~\cite{cpgrl2022}, and the clock/phase-reward methods of Siekmann et
al.~\cite{siekmann2021}, which carried Cassie to a 100\,m Guinness record (24.73\,s,
$\approx$4.1\,m/s average)~\cite{crowley2024cassie}. A literature review conducted mid-project
(\cref{sec:rl-cpg}) returned one unambiguous constraint that reshaped the architecture here:
\textbf{no published system achieves running with per-cycle-only gait-parameter updates and no
intra-cycle feedback channel} — every successful system keeps a per-step path from observation to
action. The same review supplied the phase-gated contact rewards and efficiency-term designs
adopted in \cref{sec:rl-cpg}. On the sim-to-real side, the standard toolkit — domain
randomisation with curricula, observation history in place of privileged state, explicit sensor
models — is exercised in \cref{sec:sim2real-lineage}, where an ablation shows which of its
components actually carried the cost.
