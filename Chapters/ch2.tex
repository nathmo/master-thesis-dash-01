\section{Background and literature review}
\label{sec:bg}

This chapter supplies the frame in which the choices of \cref{sec:robot,sec:rl} are positionned. 
It also fixes the vocabulary, because ``stable'' carries different meanings in this field. 
It present the historical context of bipedal locomotion and sorts theses approaches on seven analytical axes. 
Finally, it narrows to the reinforcement-learning lineage this architecture belongs to.

\subsection{Definitions}
\label{sec:bg-defs}

Each historical family attempt to achieve bipedal locomotion using different strategies. 
\Cref{tab:bg-stability} is the ladder of those concepts with the assumption they each requires.


A few terms recur :
\textbf{Underactuation} is the excess of degrees of freedom over actuators. 
\textbf{Unilateral contact} means a foot can push but not pull, and only inside the \textbf{friction cone}. 
The point where the push applies is the \textbf{centre of pressure}, confined to the contact patch. 
\textbf{Duty factor} is the fraction of a stride each foot spends in stance, and a \textbf{flight phase} is the interval with no foot down. 
The \textbf{sim-to-real gap} is the performance loss on transfer from a simulated plant to the physical one.
In the context of this thesis, \textbf{Failure} is episode termination on body contact, or on leaving the measured workspace. 
\textbf{Stability margin} is reported as a survivor count and a seed variance rather than as a scalar reward, for the reason given in \cref{sec:margin-analysis}.
The reference timescale is the free-topple time of the inverted pendulum, $\sqrt{h/g} \approx 310$\,ms for this robot.
\textbf{Stability} is the ability to continue locomotion when subjected to disturbances.

\subsection{What makes bipedal locomotion hard}
\label{sec:bg-hard}

A legged robot touches the world only through intermittent unilateral contacts. Three consequences
follow.

\textbf{It is underactuated.} Six leg motors against twelve degrees of freedom leaves six base
degrees with no motor attached. Those six can be influenced only through the ground. A flight phase
has no ground, so the centre of mass follows a ballistic arc no actuation can alter, and the robot
chooses only the posture it will land in. \Cref{sec:rl-curriculum} makes that structural: the
curriculum releases the base degrees one at a time, and the milestone that fails names the
uncontrolled mode.

\textbf{The inputs are unilateral and saturating.} The centre of pressure moves only within the
contact patch, and for a point foot the patch is a point. That leaves foot placement as the
principal input. It is chosen once per step, and bounded by the leg's reachable workspace, so the
controller holds a small number of discrete bounded opportunities to act.

\textbf{The timescales are short.} An inverted pendulum of leg length $h$ diverges with time
constant $\sqrt{h/g}$, about 310\,ms here, and running contacts last tens of milliseconds. A
correction must therefore be planned, commanded and mechanically executed inside a few tenths of a
second, which makes actuator bandwidth a first-class constraint. \Cref{sec:plant-id-drive} measures
this robot's drive at a 0.8\,Hz pole with $\tau = 199$\,ms, against that 310\,ms divergence.

Running differs from walking in kind rather than in degree. In walking the centre of mass vaults
over a stiff support leg and rises at mid-stance. In running it bounces: it falls at mid-stance as
the leg compresses, rises as the leg rebounds, and passes through a flight phase. A different
reduced model therefore applies, which is why frameworks built for walking do not extend to running
when the commanded speed is raised. Three reduced models recur below. The \textbf{inverted
pendulum} gives the divergence time above. The \textbf{spring-loaded inverted pendulum} (SLIP)
models running. The \textbf{capture point}~\cite{pratt2006capture} is the foothold that brings the
inverted pendulum to rest, and it underlies both the pitch reflex of \cref{sec:rl-reflexes} and the
scripted baseline of \cref{sec:scripted-baseline}.

\subsection{Historical context}
\label{sec:bg-history}

The history is more legible read as a sequence of answers to ``where does the stability come
from''. The six families are in active use simultaneously, and the choice among them is governed by
what the hardware can physically do. \Cref{tab:bg-approaches} carries what each buys and costs, so
the text below states only the mechanism and the anchor systems.

\textbf{Geometry.} WABOT-1 (1973) moved slowly enough for its own momentum to be
neglected~\cite{lim2007waseda}. The dynamic generalisation is the zero moment
point~\cite{vukobratovic1969,vukobratovic2004}, which carried the field for three decades through
Honda's P2 and ASIMO~\cite{hirai1998honda}. The linear inverted pendulum model and preview control
made it cheap to compute~\cite{kajita2001lipm,kajita2003preview}.

\textbf{The mechanism.} McGeer's passive dynamic walkers descend a ramp on gravity alone, with no
motors and no computer~\cite{mcgeer1990}. The gait is a limit cycle set by linkage geometry and
mass distribution. Collins et al.\ added the minimum actuation needed to walk level, and measured a
cost of transport near 0.20, comparable to a human~\cite{collins2005}.

\textbf{Elasticity.} Blickhan showed that one spring-mass model describes running across animals
spanning orders of magnitude in body mass~\cite{blickhan1989}, and Full and Koditschek framed such
models as templates with the physical machine as their anchor~\cite{full1999templates}. Series
elastic actuation~\cite{pratt1995sea} makes the template buildable, and ATRIAS is the most literal
instantiation~\cite{hubicki2016atrias}, with Cassie and Digit as its descendants. DASH-01's passive
ankle is a spring-rate commitment of the same kind, and \cref{sec:ankle-build} is what happens when
one is sized wrong.

\textbf{Foot placement.} Raibert's hoppers reframed the problem: a running robot has to be balanced
once per step rather than continuously~\cite{raibert1986}. Control decomposes into thrust at bottom
of stance, foot placement about a neutral point,
\begin{equation}
  x_f \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot x\,T_s \;+\; k\,(\dot x - \dot x_d),
  \label{eq:neutral-point}
\end{equation}
and hip torque applied during stance only. The lineage runs through
BigDog~\cite{raibert2008bigdog} to Atlas, and \cref{eq:neutral-point} appears directly in the
scripted baseline of \cref{sec:scripted-baseline}. Individual loops were later proved in simplified
models~\cite{koditschek1991,pratt2006capture}, but never their composition on a physical machine.

\textbf{A provable limit cycle.} Hybrid zero dynamics enforces virtual constraints indexed by the
robot's own progress through the step rather than by time. That collapses the closed-loop dynamics
onto a low-dimensional surface, whose orbital stability a Poincar\'e map can
certify~\cite{westervelt2003hzd,westervelt2007book}. RABBIT demonstrated it on an underactuated
biped with point feet~\cite{chevallereau2003rabbit}, and Grizzle et al.\ set out the
control-theoretic structure for the 3D case~\cite{grizzle2014}. The hybrid-systems foundation,
including impacts and periodic-orbit stability, is due to Hurmuzlu et al.~\cite{hurmuzlu2004}. The
family also supplies the idea of indexing a gait by phase rather than by time, which every modern
learned architecture reuses.

\textbf{Re-solving online.} Wieber recast zero-moment-point walking as model-predictive
control~\cite{wieber2006mpc}. Kuindersma et al.\ built the whole-body version that ran Atlas at the
DARPA Robotics Challenge~\cite{kuindersma2016atlas}, and Di Carlo et al.\ produced the convex
formulation now underlying most classical legged stacks~\cite{dicarlo2018mpc}. Decisively for this
thesis, the output is a force command that must reach the ground essentially unfiltered, so the
family demands high-bandwidth torque control.

\textbf{Experience.} A policy, typically a small network, is optimised by reinforcement learning to
maximise a reward over millions of simulated episodes. Planner, whole-body controller and joint
tracker collapse into one evaluation. Heess et al.\ showed that rich terrain and a simple reward
produce running gaits with no gait prior~\cite{heess2017emergence}. PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo}
became the workhorse, MuJoCo~\cite{todorov2012mujoco} made the sample cost affordable, and
massively parallel simulation cut wall-clock further~\cite{rudin2021}. Transfer is the hard part,
and the toolkit is domain randomisation~\cite{tobin2017dr,tan2018sim2real}, learned actuator
models~\cite{hwangbo2019anymal} and teacher--student
distillation~\cite{lee2020terrain,miki2022perceptive}. On bipeds, Xie et al.\ carried iterative
sim-to-real design onto Cassie~\cite{xie2019}, Siekmann et al.\ demonstrated the full gait range
through periodic reward composition~\cite{siekmann2021}, Radosavovic et al.\ carried the approach
to a full humanoid~\cite{radosavovic2024humanoid}, and Crowley et al.\ set the current 100\,m
record~\cite{crowley2024cassie}.

Two observations close the survey. The families are complementary rather than sequential, and most
working systems combine them. A typical modern system is a learned policy over a phase variable
borrowed from hybrid zero dynamics, with a spring borrowed from SLIP, on hardware descended from
Raibert's. This thesis is no exception. And across fifty years the stability burden transfers from
the controller to the robot and to the model. That is what makes measurement, rather than control
design, the critical path for a project like this one.

\subsection{Approach comparison}
\label{sec:bg-axes}

The axes below are the analysis, and a system takes a position on
each independently. \Cref{tab:bg-eras} sorts the families by row and axis by column.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Dynamic model complexity}, from kinematics through centroidal and full rigid-body
        to learned. The trade-off is fidelity against tractability.
  \item \textbf{Stability concept}, from \cref{tab:bg-stability}.
  \item \textbf{Where the intelligence sits}: fully model-based, model-based plus online
        optimisation, hierarchical, or end-to-end learned. Learning has not replaced model-based
        control in the systems cited above. Atlas~\cite{kuindersma2016atlas} and MIT
        Cheetah~\cite{dicarlo2018mpc} are optimisation-based, and
        Cassie~\cite{siekmann2021,crowley2024cassie} pairs a learned policy with an authored clock.
  \item \textbf{Actuation interface}: position, velocity, torque or impedance. A position servo is
        simple and robust, but it folds the actuator dynamics into the plant and gives poor
        contact-force authority. Torque is the prerequisite for momentum and force control.
        Impedance is the intermediate category, and the one this thesis ends up wanting
        (\cref{sec:rl-impedance}).
  \item \textbf{Bandwidth and temporal hierarchy}: task planning at 1 to 10\,Hz, gait and
        model-predictive layers at 10 to 100\,Hz, whole-body control at 100 to 1000\,Hz, and the
        current loop at 1 to 20\,kHz. \emph{Command-loop rate is not actuator bandwidth}, and the
        two are routinely conflated. This project runs a 200\,Hz policy against a measured 0.8\,Hz
        drive pole (\cref{sec:plant-id-drive}), and that ratio is where the central finding sits.
  \item \textbf{Contact treatment}, from a fixed contact assumption to learned contact behaviour.
        Relevance rises from flat walking to running.
  \item \textbf{Source of the gait}: hand-authored, analytic, trajectory optimisation, motion
        imitation~\cite{peng2018deepmimic}, learning with a reference, or reference-free learning.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Learning for locomotion: lineage and trade-offs}
\label{sec:bg-rl-loco}

Within the learned family, the relevant lineage is \emph{periodically parameterised} action spaces,
in which the policy shapes a periodic generator rather than emitting joint targets. Policies
Modulating Trajectory Generators established the pattern~\cite{pmtg2018}. Oscillator-based CPG-RL
instantiated it with coupled phase oscillators~\cite{ijspeert2008,cpgrl2022}, and the clock and
phase-reward methods of Siekmann et al.~\cite{siekmann2021} carried Cassie to the 100\,m
record~\cite{crowley2024cassie}. The network no longer has to discover that locomotion is periodic,
only which periodic signal it wants.

\textbf{The basis is not the contribution.} A band-limited periodic gait parameterisation predates
this work by more than a decade. Truncated Fourier series ran on RoboCup humanoids from 2009, with
coefficients fixed offline by particle swarm optimisation~\cite{shafii2010tfs}, and Yang et
al.~\cite{yang2010tfs} combined the same formulation with reinforcement learning. Rhythmic dynamic
movement primitives carry a Fourier-type basis over phase~\cite{ijspeert2013dmp}, Fourier Latent
Dynamics parameterises periodic motion in a Fourier latent~\cite{fld2024}, Wang et
al.~\cite{wang2023fourier} plan a Fourier gait offline, and Ates and Morg\"ul learn a
frequency-domain generator from motion capture~\cite{ates2025freqgait}. The nearest neighbour is
Castillo et al.~\cite{castillo2020hzdrl}, whose policy emits the B\'ezier coefficients of
hybrid-zero-dynamics virtual constraints directly. \textbf{Emitting basis coefficients as the
action is therefore theirs, not this thesis's.} What is added here is three narrower things. The
mid-cycle rewrite is explicitly priced rather than free. The gait channel and the fast-feedback
channel are separated and billed differently (\cref{sec:rl-cpg}). And the choice of basis is
settled by a measured A/B on identical plants and rewards (\cref{sec:cpg-ab}).

\textbf{The periodicity prior is close to universal, and systems differ in where they put it.} It
can go in the \emph{reward}~\cite{siekmann2021}, in a \emph{reference}~\cite{peng2018deepmimic}, in
an authored \emph{generator} the policy modulates~\cite{pmtg2018,cpgrl2022}, or in the \emph{action
space} itself~\cite{castillo2020hzdrl}. Only the last makes the shape of one cycle a decision
variable at run time. \Cref{tab:prior-axes} sorts on placement, and separates two axes a single
label hides: the \emph{intra-limb} trajectory one leg follows, and the \emph{inter-limb} phase
relation between legs.

A \textbf{shape prior} fixes the trajectory and exposes its timing, so tuning does not enlarge the
reachable set. A \textbf{structural prior} constrains the class and leaves the member free, so the
reachable set grows with the parameterisation. DASH-01 frees the intra-limb axis because it has no
template to copy. Hardware calibration established that the cam is a full crank, sweeping about
$245^{\circ}$ against a CAD-guessed $\pm86^{\circ}$ (\cref{sec:hw-experiments}), and the 4-bar's
transmission ratio varies strongly with posture (\cref{sec:hw-force-map}). No animal offers a gait
for that morphology. The mirror on the inter-limb axis is a shape prior, taken because the task is
a straight-line dash and because it halves the emitted specification to 14 coefficients. Its cost
was paid later: a mirror-symmetric gait travels only in a straight line, so steering had to be
added as two explicit asymmetry channels.

Three trade-offs follow from the lineage, and the thesis lives on all three.

\textbf{Sim-to-real gap.} Ranked as measured on this robot rather than as usually listed, the
sources are actuator dynamics and drive bandwidth, mass and inertia error, the contact and friction
model, latency, sensor noise, and unmodelled compliance. The standard toolkit is domain
randomisation with curricula, observation history in place of privileged state, explicit sensor and
delay models, and actuator networks~\cite{hwangbo2019anymal}. The useful framing is randomisation
as robustness training over a distribution of plants, rather than as noise injection. This thesis
measures first, and randomises around the measurement (\cref{sec:sim2real-lineage}). The
counter-lesson is that randomising around an optimistic plant does not recover the plant error. No
policy has run on the hardware, so that is an inference from the plant-correction table rather than
a transfer result.

\textbf{Learning efficiency and dimensionality reduction.} The raw problem is a torque sequence in
$\mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{joint}} \times T}$, and the structured problem is 10 to 50 gait-morphology
parameters. The prior asserted is that successful locomotion is approximately periodic,
band-limited and bounded. The reduction removes exploration rather than imposing answers. It costs
a reachable set bounded by the harmonic count, no stability guarantee from smoothness, and the need
for a fast feedback channel outside the generator. Modern results use $10^8$ to $10^9$ transitions,
with thousands of parallel environments~\cite{rudin2021}. A curriculum optimal at that budget is
not necessarily optimal at this project's, so sample efficiency is the actual research variable
here.

\textbf{Curriculum design.} Published curricula range over command, terrain, disturbance and
randomisation, usually on an already fully dynamic robot, and the recent shift is toward
performance-adaptive schedules. This thesis instead applies difficulty to the
\emph{controllability of the plant} (\cref{sec:rl-curriculum}), by releasing base degrees of
freedom one at a time. It is weaker than the state of the art on every other axis: binary rather
than continuous release, hand-scheduled rather than performance-gated advance, and a single
difficulty axis rather than a vector. The known danger is that a strongly constrained plant lets
the policy learn that a suppressed mode does not exist, so release then produces immediate failure.

\subsection{Platforms, and where DASH-01 sits}
\label{sec:bg-platforms}

The bipeds producing the results above fall into three groups, separated more by actuation and
budget than by control philosophy. \textbf{Series-elastic research bipeds} such as
ATRIAS~\cite{hubicki2016atrias} and its descendants Cassie and Digit carry leaf springs in series
with the drives, and a high-voltage bus. They hold the speed record~\cite{crowley2024cassie}, and
they are the product of a decade of institutional effort. \textbf{Quasi-direct-drive robots in the
MIT Cheetah lineage}~\cite{seok2015design,wensing2017proprioceptive,katz2019minicheetah} are
predominantly quadrupeds, and architecturally the most influential group. Their pattern is
quasi-direct-drive actuation commanded in torque mode, adopted by essentially every recent legged
platform. \textbf{Hobby-class humanoids} are built from position-controlled smart servos, and are
confined to the static-walking regime for a structural reason rather than a budget one. A servo
closing its own position loop cannot deliver a commanded ground force, so the dynamic families stay
unavailable whatever controller sits above them.

DASH-01 sits deliberately between the second and third groups: quasi-direct-drive actuators and a
running-oriented morphology, on a student budget and a single-board computer. Three bets define it.
A parallel 4-bar knee moves the knee actuator's mass proximally. A passive ankle spring replaces a
seventh and eighth motor. Six motors do the work of ten. \Cref{tab:speed-ceiling} confirms the
first bet, at $-0.93$\,(m/s)/kg for distal mass against $-0.11$ for the torso.
\Cref{sec:hw-force-map} shows the second bet was wrong by a factor of eight. The third stands.

What separates this platform from its neighbours is not any single choice. It is that the drive
electronics were bought as a bundle with the actuator. That put the robot in the third group's
actuation interface, while its mechanics belong to the second. \Cref{sec:plant-id-drive} measures
what it costs.

\subsection{The gap, and three hypotheses}
\label{sec:bg-gap}

Published structured-action-space results come from platforms with high-bandwidth torque control.
What survives on a low-bandwidth, position-servo, low-cost plant is not documented, and that is the
gap this work sits in. The question is therefore not why to use reinforcement learning. It is
\textbf{at what level learning should sit, given this robot's actuator dynamics and available
control bandwidth}. Learning to joint targets through a servo, and learning to torque through an
actuator, are different learning problems, because the first forces the policy through the
closed-loop servo dynamics.

Three hypotheses follow. \Cref{sec:results} tests them, and \cref{sec:discussion} revisits them.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{H1, dimensionality reduction.} A Fourier gait-morphology action space converges in
        fewer environment steps than joint-level action spaces on this plant.
  \item \textbf{H2, control authority.} Gait morphology plus phase-dependent impedance gives a
        sufficient basin of attraction, without direct per-step residual joint control.
  \item \textbf{H3, robustness.} The structured controller degrades less across the sim-to-real
        boundary than an unconstrained one.
\end{itemize}

The scoping is uneven, and is stated as such. H1 is answered by the attempt sequence of
\cref{sec:rl-ladder}. H2 is answered only partially, because the impedance interface was never
available on the hardware. H3 is answered only in simulation, since no policy has run on the robot.
