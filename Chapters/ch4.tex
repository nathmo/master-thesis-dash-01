\section{Results}

\subsection{Hardware}

The delivered hardware platform: a 15.14\,kg biped as weighed — 14.72\,kg after the
carbon-ankle rebuild (\cref{sec:rebuilt-robot}) — with six quasi-direct-drive
actuators (\cref{tab:motors}), a 13S2P / 480\,Wh / 250\,A pack (\cref{sec:hw-battery}), a
Raspberry Pi 3B closing a 200\,Hz control loop at 3\,\% late ticks on stock Linux with half its
budget spare (\cref{tab:loop-timing,tab:deploy-ab}), dual 1\,Mbit CAN at 26\,\% utilisation, an
IMU characterised to provenance-labelled noise figures (\cref{tab:imu}), and a measured plant
model whose every correction is tabulated in \cref{tab:plant-id}. The capability analysis on the
corrected plant places the machine at $\approx$2.7\,m/s credible burst and $\approx$1.3\,m/s
thermally sustained (\cref{sec:speed-ceiling-corrected}). The deployment stack — calibration,
safety guards, flight recorder — runs on the robot under systemd and has survived (and
diagnosed) its first real incident (\cref{sec:deploy}).

\subsection{RL experiments}

\Cref{tab:rl-results} remains the headline: the milestone ladder culminating in a 6-DOF-free
plant that runs 60\,s without falling at 2.6\,m/s, and the cadence-fixed m7 at 2.71\,m/s —
$\approx$70\,\% of the (pre-correction) credible ceiling. Around it, the consolidated findings:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Action space}: direct torque never converged and was undeployable as assumed
        (\cref{sec:rl-torque}; premises later partially overturned by measurement); per-step
        position targets converged to standing but skated (\cref{sec:rl-position}); the
        per-step-reparameterised Fourier CPG with reflexes and residuals is the design that
        works (\cref{sec:rl-cpg}).
  \item \textbf{Generator A/B}: oscillator-CPG vs Fourier is a measured tie with complementary
        strengths; the oscillator produced the single best controller (the only one that
        executes the sprint's stop phase) but walls at the roll DOF where Fourier passes
        (\cref{sec:cpg-ab}).
  \item \textbf{The pitch wall is the plant's}: identical $\sim$1\,s failures for RL and for a
        21-gain classical controller, equal to the analytic free-topple time across a 5$\times$
        gravity sweep (\cref{sec:scripted-baseline}); broken in simulation only by a
        12$\times$ ankle stiffening (\cref{sec:rl-pitch-wall}); and it survives the hardware
        drive retune to 3\,Hz (\cref{sec:rebuilt-robot}) — structural, not bandwidth.
  \item \textbf{On the rebuilt plant} (carbon ankle, 3\,Hz drive, velocity caps), the scripted
        controller demonstrates the first full command-following mission — stand, walk forward,
        walk backward, stand again — 35\,s, 3/3 seeds, drive delivery 0.98
        (\cref{sec:rebuilt-robot}).
  \item \textbf{On the measured plant}, the sim-to-real lineage completes the descent to the
        real 0.8\,Hz drive with a 3$\times$ score gain over its parent, and its diagnosis
        history (steering asymmetry, DR ablation, command-range fit) is itself a result
        (\cref{sec:sim2real-lineage}).
  \item \textbf{The ankle question closed as a build decision}: statics eliminate the as-built
        soft spring, learning selects the compression-capable strut, and buckling arithmetic
        rejects the fabricated part in favour of carbon (\cref{sec:ankle-build}).
\end{itemize}
