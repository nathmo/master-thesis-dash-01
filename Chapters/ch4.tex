\section{Results}

\subsection{Hardware}

The delivered hardware platform is a biped weighed at 15.14\,kg, or 14.72\,kg after the carbon-ankle
rebuild (\cref{sec:rebuilt-robot}). It carries six quasi-direct-drive actuators
(\cref{tab:motors}) and a 13S2P pack of 480\,Wh rated to 250\,A peak (\cref{sec:hw-battery}). A
Raspberry Pi 3B closes a 200\,Hz control loop on stock Linux, at 3\,\% late ticks with half its
budget spare (\cref{tab:loop-timing,tab:deploy-ab}). Two 1\,Mbit CAN buses run at 26\,\%
utilisation. The IMU is characterised to provenance-labelled noise figures (\cref{tab:imu}), and
\cref{tab:plant-id} tabulates every correction in the measured plant model. The capability analysis
on the corrected plant places the robot at about 2.7\,m/s credible burst and about 1.3\,m/s
thermally sustained (\cref{sec:speed-ceiling-corrected}). The deployment stack — calibration,
safety guards and flight recorder — runs on the robot under systemd, and it has survived and
diagnosed its first real incident (\cref{sec:deploy}).

\subsection{RL experiments}

\Cref{tab:rl-results} remains the headline. The milestone ladder culminates in a 6-DOF-free plant
that runs 60\,s without falling at 2.6\,m/s, and in the cadence-fixed m7 at 2.71\,m/s. The latter
is about 70\,\% of the credible ceiling as it stood before the plant correction. The consolidated
findings sit around that result.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Action space.} Direct torque never converged, and it was judged undeployable on
        premises that measurement later partly overturned (\cref{sec:rl-torque}). Per-step
        position targets converged to standing but skated (\cref{sec:rl-position}). The Fourier
        CPG, re-parameterised every step and wrapped in reflexes and residuals, is the design that
        works (\cref{sec:rl-cpg}).
  \item \textbf{Generator A/B.} The oscillator CPG and the Fourier series tie by measurement, with
        complementary strengths. The oscillator produced the single best controller, and the only
        one that executes the sprint's stop phase. It walls at the roll degree of freedom, where
        the Fourier generator passes (\cref{sec:cpg-ab}).
  \item \textbf{The pitch wall belongs to the plant.} RL and a 21-gain classical controller fail
        identically at about 1\,s, which equals the analytic free-topple time across a 5$\times$
        gravity sweep (\cref{sec:scripted-baseline}). Only a 12$\times$ ankle stiffening breaks it
        in simulation (\cref{sec:rl-pitch-wall}). It survives the hardware drive retune to 3\,Hz
        (\cref{sec:rebuilt-robot}), which makes it structural rather than bandwidth-limited.
  \item \textbf{On the rebuilt plant}, with the carbon ankle, the 3\,Hz drive and velocity caps,
        the scripted controller demonstrates the first full command-following mission: stand, walk
        forward, walk backward, stand again. It runs 35\,s on 3 of 3 seeds, at a drive delivery of
        0.98 (\cref{sec:rebuilt-robot}).
  \item \textbf{On the measured plant}, the sim-to-real lineage completes the descent to the real
        0.8\,Hz drive, with a 3$\times$ score gain over its parent. Its diagnosis history is
        itself a result, covering the steering asymmetry, the domain-randomisation ablation and
        the command-range fit (\cref{sec:sim2real-lineage}).
  \item \textbf{The ankle question closed as a build decision.} Statics eliminate the as-built
        soft spring. Learning selects the compression-capable strut. Buckling arithmetic rejects
        the fabricated part in favour of carbon (\cref{sec:ankle-build}).
\end{itemize}
