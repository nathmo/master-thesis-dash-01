\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Successes}

The pipeline exists end to end, and every stage has been validated at least once. CAD exports to a
loop-closed MJCF that matches the measured workspace at 100\,\% of poses. A capability analysis
independently reproduces both the high-cadence optimum and the ankle-stiffness threshold that RL
discovered. An action-space design turned a non-converging problem into a reliably trainable one,
and an independent generator A/B confirmed that twice. A curriculum mechanism (base-DOF release,
with rigid rails and a constant observation and action shape) carries warm starts across plants. A
measurement campaign corrected the plant, using instruments built on the robot itself. And a
deployment stack proved the value of its flight recorder within an hour of installation. The
standing policy on the fully free plant (60 seconds, zero falls, 2.6\,m/s) is a real result on a
hard plant. The drivable lineage's survival of the descent to the measured 0.8\,Hz drive is the
strongest sim-to-real evidence in the record.

\subsection{Failures}

Direct torque control failed twice over, on convergence and on assumed deployability. Abandoning it
was itself partly wrong, because the measured premises now point back toward current-mode control
with the PD closed on the Pi. Fully autonomous untethered running was not reached. The sprint
policies observe privileged state, the drivable lineage is not yet robust, and no policy has run on
the robot. The record ambition is dead on this chassis by measurement, at about 1.3\,m/s sustained
against a 4\,m/s record pace. Three further failures stand out. The roll milestone remains the RL
ladder's weak link. The 6\,Hz footfall chatter survives every post-hoc lever, and awaits a
from-scratch retrain with cadence pressure applied from step zero. And the physical robot's left
leg was lost to a calibration failure before any policy reached it, which is the clearest possible
demonstration that deployment infrastructure is not overhead.

\subsection{Lessons learned}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Optimisers exploit objectives, and neural networks are not the culprit.} PPO gamed
        the reward by skating, penetrating the floor, shuttling and committing suicide by penalty.
        The scripted baseline's coordinate-descent tuner gamed its own objective identically.
        Every objective needs adversarial review before its optimiser gets it.
  \item \textbf{The stability-margin advantage of learning is conditional, and the conditions are
        measurable.} The theoretical case for RL on an unstable plant is that the hand-designed
        controller lies inside the policy class, so the learned optimum can only match or exceed
        it (\cref{sec:bg-margin}). Three premises carry that argument, and this project paid none
        of them in full. The plant was not exact enough, because every parameter moved
        (\cref{tab:plant-id}). The reward did not price distance from the failure boundary; it
        priced survival and distance, so the optimiser parked on the boundary. And the training
        budget did not reach the asymptote, because 600\,M steps per milestone is large rather
        than asymptotic. Hence the m2 result, where a margin existed and the learning did not find
        it. The generalisable diagnostic is that \textbf{seed variance is the margin measurement}.
        A figure of 20.3\,$\pm$\,21.6\,s against 5 of 5 at the episode cap is not a worse mean. It
        is bimodality, and a learning curve plotting the mean will not show it. At m3 the
        comparison is vacuous rather than adverse. With an empty feasible set, every controller
        drawn from it performs identically, and RL optimises over controllers, not over physics
        (\cref{sec:margin-analysis}).
  \item \textbf{The useful question about a gait prior is which kind, not how much.} A shape prior
        fixes the trajectory and exposes its timing. Tuning it does not enlarge the reachable set,
        so it is safe only when the morphology has a natural template to copy. A structural prior
        constrains the class and leaves the member free, and its reachable set grows with the
        parameterisation. DASH-01 is the case that separates the two, because its cam turned out
        to be a full crank with no biological analogue. The cadence agreement between the learned
        optimum and the independent physics sweep is the evidence that the wider space was
        genuinely used (\cref{sec:bg-prior}). The prior never vanished. It moved into the action
        space, and stayed hand-wired at the one channel where feedback must not be blind. Neither
        the Fourier basis nor the practice of emitting basis coefficients as actions is new
        (\cref{sec:bg-prior}); what the two-axis reading buys is a way to compare systems that are
        usually filed under a single label.
  \item \textbf{Every assumed plant parameter measured later moved pessimistically}: mass by
        $+18\%$, torque by $-15\%$, drive bandwidth by $-16\times$, plus friction and the ankle.
        Measure before training, not after. And when a measurement lands, apply it as \emph{the}
        plant rather than as an optional variant, or the wrong default gets trained on.
  \item \textbf{Simulation results need the same scepticism as rewards.} Soft joint limits,
        constraints inactive at settle, frame-relative safety checks, stale keyframes, a
        solver-version split, and an actuator-velocity cap that was simply never present each
        produced a clean, converged, wrong result. The last of those let a prized policy
        ``balance'' at 23\,rad/s on a 22\,rad/s motor. Self-tests, smoke gates or measurement
        caught each one.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation hygiene is a first-class deliverable.} Six rules earned their place:
        evaluate with training aids forced off, after three separate deceptions by curves with a
        crutch; evaluate on a fixed task across curricula; use at least 20 seeded episodes; use
        paired seeds and survivor counts under bimodality; report peak rather than final under
        one-way curricula; and treat \texttt{curriculum.json} as ground truth on restore. Four
        separate evaluation bugs each nearly discarded or crowned the wrong policy.
  \item \textbf{Curricula need three rules.} Gate the advance, not just the opening. Never gate a
        rescue ramp on the competence it is meant to rescue. And diff configurations against the
        last working run before theorising.
  \item \textbf{A parameter range widened ``to be non-binding'' can become the operating point.} A
        neutral network output decodes to the middle of the range, which is what produced the
        25\,Hz gait-clock bug. A comment asserting an unexercised assumption also stays wrong for
        months.
  \item \textbf{Hardware integration dominates, and instrumentation pays for itself.} The
        measurement instrument found the drive ceiling. The flight recorder found a daemon-killing
        crash and a dead bus within its first hour. And the trap of the measurement disturbing the
        measured was walked into twice: once with the IMU on a swaying rig, once with the loop
        rate under SSH load. The second time produced a committed wrong conclusion that only an
        A/B retracted.
  \item \textbf{Progressive validation works.} The base-DOF ladder localised failures to single
        degrees of freedom. The toy problem debugged the deploy chain at one degree of freedom.
        Telemetry cross-checks and the workspace-as-safety-net made hardware sessions survivable,
        and the one incident occurred precisely in the gap the guard now closes.
\end{enumerate}
