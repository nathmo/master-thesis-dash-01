\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This chapter reports what exists, measured. It separates four things: what the hardware delivers,
what the policies do in simulation, what the three controlled comparisons say, and which of those
results survive the plant correction of \cref{sec:plant-id}. That last separation is the
sim-to-real argument, so it gets its own table rather than being left implicit.

\subsection{Hardware delivered}

The platform is a biped weighed at 15.14\,kg, or 14.72\,kg after the carbon-ankle rebuild. It
carries six quasi-direct-drive actuators (\cref{tab:motors}). Its 13S2P pack holds 468\,Wh and is
rated 100\,A continuous and 184\,A for 10\,s (\cref{sec:hw-battery}). A Raspberry Pi 3B closes a
200\,Hz control loop on stock Linux, at 3\,\% late ticks with half its budget spare
(\cref{tab:loop-timing}). Two 1\,Mbit CAN buses run at 26\,\% utilisation. The inertial sensor is
characterised to provenance-labelled noise figures, and \cref{tab:plant-id} tabulates every
correction in the measured plant model. The deployment stack runs under \texttt{systemd}, and it
has diagnosed its first real incident (\cref{sec:deploy-blackbox}).

The capability analysis on the corrected plant places the mechanism at about \textbf{2.7\,m/s
credible burst and 1.3\,m/s thermally sustained} (\cref{tab:speed-ceiling}). The static force map
places the actuators at a median 4.30\,BW of vertical toe capacity over the running stance region.
They clear the 3.5\,BW requirement over 77\,\% of that region. The passive ankle gives a median
0.42\,BW, and clears the requirement over 0.0\,\% of it (\cref{sec:hw-force-map}).

At the time of writing the right leg is fully functional under the 200\,Hz stack, measured at
188\,Hz. The left leg awaits repair of the joint lost on 2026-08-10. \textbf{No trained policy has
run on the robot.} That statement is narrower than ``nothing has run on hardware''. The
hand-authored fixed gait plays on the real motors. The web interface drives all six joints under
safety limits. Every measurement in \cref{sec:plant-id} came off the machine. What has never
happened is a learned controller closing a loop on the physical robot.

\textbf{The fully free 6-DOF plant runs and does not fall.} That is 60 seconds of simulated
running, at the episode limit, with zero variance across evaluation episodes, at 2.6\,m/s. Two
caveats belong next to it. Milestone \texttt{m6} balances but runs \emph{through} the finish line
without executing the stop, which is a task-completion defect rather than a balance one. And
\texttt{m5} still topples on a fraction of episodes.

Against the pre-correction ceiling of \cref{tab:speed-ceiling}, 2.6\,m/s is about 70\,\% of the
credible-cadence figure, and already above the thermally sustainable one. On the corrected plant it
sits essentially at the burst ceiling, and at twice the sustained figure.

\Cref{fig:ladder} shows the shape behind those numbers, and one feature of it is a result in its
own right. The training curves for \texttt{m3} to \texttt{m6} sit between 8 and 15\,s. The
deterministic evaluations of the same checkpoints reach 26 to 60\,s. The exploration noise that
separates them is why rule 1 of \cref{sec:rl-eval} exists. No claim in this thesis rests on a
training curve alone.

\paragraph{Action-space verdicts, one line each.} Direct torque never converged, and was abandoned
on premises that measurement later overturned (\cref{sec:rl-torque}). Per-step position targets
converged to standing but skated (\cref{sec:rl-position}). Impedance was designed and never run,
because the drive mode it needs was never switched on the hardware (\cref{sec:rl-impedance}). The
Fourier gait generator is the design that works (\cref{sec:rl-cpg}). Its reflex and residual
channels are necessary rather than decorative, because removing the per-step channel produces
stability without locomotion (\cref{sec:cpg-ab}).

\subsection{Three controlled comparisons}

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figures/fig_experiments.pdf}
\caption{Three comparisons, each run with one variable changed and everything else held fixed.
\textbf{(a)} Generator A/B from a cold start on the pitch-free plant. The oscillator is more
sample-efficient cold, at an asymptote near 2.0\,s against 1.2\,s. The Fourier arm is the more
seed-reliable of the two on warm starts (\cref{sec:cpg-ab}). \textbf{(b)} Drive bandwidth on a cold
start at m2, truncated to the shortest arm's budget. The robot as built, at 0.8\,Hz, is the outlier
and never walks. The retuned 3\,Hz plant recovers most of the idealised 12\,Hz arm
(\cref{sec:rebuilt-robot}). Episode length does not capture that the 12\,Hz arm folds a leg into
the workspace kill. \textbf{(c)} Ankle variants on a cold start at m3 on the measured plant. The
compression-capable carbon strut and the welded ankle climb. The tension-only rod and a spring at
$k = 350$ stay pinned at the free-fall time of about 1\,s (\cref{sec:ankle-build}).}
\label{fig:experiments}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The pitch wall belongs to the plant.} Learning and a 21-gain scripted controller fail
identically at about 1\,s. That equals the analytic free-topple time across a 5$\times$ gravity
sweep (\cref{tab:freefall}). Only a 12$\times$ ankle stiffening breaks it in simulation. It
survives the hardware drive retune to 3\,Hz, which makes it structural rather than
bandwidth-limited. On m2, where a margin does exist, the scripted controller runs 60\,s on 5 of 5
seeds against the learned reference's 20.3\,$\pm$\,21.6\,s. It wins on the variance rather than on
the mean (\cref{sec:margin-analysis}).

\paragraph{The rebuilt plant walks under command.} With the carbon ankle, the 3\,Hz drive and
velocity caps, the scripted controller demonstrates the first full command-following mission. It
stands, walks forward, walks backward and stands again, in one 35\,s episode on 3 of 3 seeds, at a
drive-delivery ratio of 0.98.

\paragraph{The measured plant is trainable if the drive is ramped.} The drivable lineage completes
the descent to the real 0.8\,Hz drive, and stabilises at an episode length of 4938 and a reward of
$+3271$, three times its parent's score (\cref{sec:sim2real-lineage}). Its diagnosis history is
itself a result. It covers the steering asymmetry, the domain-randomisation ablation at
19.8$\times$ mean time between falls, and the command-range fit.

\subsection{What survives the plant correction}

\Cref{sec:plant-id} invalidates the plant every number in \cref{tab:rl-results} was measured on.
Stating which conclusions depend on the plant is more useful than deleting the table, because that
distinction \emph{is} the sim-to-real argument.

\centering
\small

\midrule

\addlinespace
The reward-exploit register, and the finding that reward curves cannot certify a gait. & The claim that the fully free plant runs and does not fall. It ran, on an ankle that will not be built. \\

\addlinespace

\addlinespace

\bottomrule

methodological results transfer and the performance results do not.}
\label{tab:survives}

That pattern was expensive to learn. Roughly 3 billion environment steps of training were spent on
a robot 18\,\% too light and 18\,\% too strong. Its ankle spring was an order of magnitude too
stiff, and its actuator an order of magnitude too fast.

\subsection{Recorded runs}
\label{sec:results-media}

Rendered rollouts accompany the results above. Each is named here, so that a claim can be checked
against a specific recording rather than against a description. All of them are \emph{simulation}.
No footage of a learned controller on the physical robot exists, for the reason given at the head
of this chapter.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \texttt{m4\_stiff\_600M}, \texttt{m5\_stiff\_600M}, \texttt{m6\_stiff\_600M}: the milestone
        ladder of \cref{tab:rl-results}, greedy evaluation, one seed each.
  \item \texttt{m3\_CPG}, \texttt{m4\_CPG}: the oscillator arm of the generator A/B
        (\cref{sec:cpg-ab}), on identical plants and rewards.
  \item \texttt{walk\_fwd\_easy\_s0}, \texttt{teleop\_easy\_m6}: the drivable lineage on the
        measured 0.8\,Hz drive (\cref{sec:sim2real-lineage}).
  \item \texttt{scripted\_m2}, \texttt{scripted\_m3\_fail}: the classical baseline succeeding at m2
        and hitting the pitch wall at m3 (\cref{sec:scripted-baseline}).
\end{itemize}
