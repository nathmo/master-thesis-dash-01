\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Successes}

The pipeline exists end to end and each stage has been validated at least once: CAD to a
loop-closed MJCF that matches the measured workspace at 100\,\% of poses; a capability analysis
that independently reproduces both the RL-discovered high-cadence optimum and the
RL-discovered ankle-stiffness threshold; an action-space design that turned a non-converging
problem into a reliably trainable one, twice confirmed by an independent generator A/B; a
curriculum mechanism (base-DOF release with rigid rails and constant observation/action shape)
that carries warm starts across plants; a measurement campaign that corrected the plant with
instruments built on the robot itself; and a deployment stack whose flight recorder proved its
value within an hour of installation. The standing policy on the fully free plant — 60 seconds,
zero falls, 2.6\,m/s — is a real result on a hard plant, and the drivable lineage's survival of
the descent to the measured 0.8\,Hz drive is the strongest sim-to-real evidence in the record.

\subsection{Failures}

Direct torque control failed twice over (convergence and assumed deployability) and its
abandonment was itself partially wrong — the measured premises now point back toward
current-mode control with the PD on the Pi. Fully autonomous untethered running was not reached:
the sprint policies observe privileged state, the drivable lineage is not yet robust, no policy
has run on the robot, and the record ambition is dead on this chassis by measurement
($\approx$1.3\,m/s sustained against a 4\,m/s record pace). The roll milestone remains the RL
ladder's weak link; the 6\,Hz footfall chatter survives every post-hoc lever and awaits a
from-scratch retrain with cadence pressure from step zero; and the left leg of the physical
robot was lost to a calibration failure before any policy reached it — the clearest possible
demonstration that deployment infrastructure is not overhead.

\subsection{Lessons learned}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Optimisers exploit objectives; neural networks are not the culprit.} The reward
        was gamed by PPO (skating, floor penetration, shuttling, penalty-suicide), and the
        scripted baseline's coordinate-descent tuner gamed its objective identically. Every
        objective needs adversarial review before its optimiser gets it.
  \item \textbf{Every assumed plant parameter measured later moved pessimistically} — mass
        $+18\%$, torque $-15\%$, drive bandwidth $-16\times$, friction, the ankle. Measure
        before training, not after; and when a measurement lands, apply it as \emph{the} plant,
        not as an optional variant (the wrong default will otherwise be trained on).
  \item \textbf{Simulation results need the same scepticism as rewards}: soft joint limits,
        constraints inactive at settle, frame-relative safety checks, stale keyframes, a
        solver-version split, and an actuator-velocity cap that was simply never present (letting
        a prized policy ``balance'' at 23\,rad/s on a 22\,rad/s motor) each produced a clean,
        converged, wrong result before being caught by self-tests, smoke gates, or measurement.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation hygiene is a first-class deliverable}: aid-off evaluation (three
        deceptions by training curves with a crutch), fixed-task evaluation across curricula,
        $\geq$20 seeded episodes, paired seeds with survivor counts under bimodality, peak-not-final
        under one-way curricula, and \texttt{curriculum.json} as ground truth for restore. Four
        separate evaluation bugs each nearly discarded or crowned the wrong policy.
  \item \textbf{Curricula: gate the advance, not just the opening; never gate a rescue ramp on
        the competence it is meant to rescue; and diff configs against the last working run
        before theorising.}
  \item \textbf{A parameter range widened ``to be non-binding'' can become the operating point}
        — the neutral network output decodes to the middle of the range (the 25\,Hz gait-clock
        bug), and a comment asserting an unexercised assumption stays wrong for months.
  \item \textbf{Hardware integration dominates, and instrumentation pays for itself}: the
        measurement instrument found the drive ceiling; the flight recorder found a
        daemon-killing crash and a dead bus in its first hour; and the measurement-disturbs-
        the-measured trap was walked into twice (IMU on a swaying rig, loop rate under SSH
        load) — the second time producing a committed wrong conclusion that only an A/B
        retracted.
  \item \textbf{Progressive validation works.} The base-DOF ladder localised failures to single
        degrees of freedom; the toy problem debugged the deploy chain at one DOF; L0-style
        telemetry cross-checks and the workspace-as-safety-net made hardware sessions survivable
        — with the one incident occurring precisely in the gap the guard now closes.
\end{enumerate}
