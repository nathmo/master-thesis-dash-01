\subsection{Hardware delivered}

Firstly, this thesis was quite involved in term of hardware and altough the mechanical design was aldready done and provided, a lot of work was required to bring it from a fancy paper weight to a robot that could be controlled.
The platform is a biped weighed at 15\,kg. It carries four AKE90-8 QDD actuators (\cref{tab:motors}) and two AK60-39 for the hip roll.
Its 13S2P pack holds 468\,Wh and is rated 100\,A continuous and 360\,A for 2\,s (\cref{sec:hw-battery}).
A Raspberry Pi 3B run the control loop at 200\,Hz on a stock Linux. The platform can be backdriven and playback a recorded gait along with running the inference for a given policy.
The platform was characterised to bound the performance envelope and to inform the training.

\subsection{Running speed ceiling estimation}

The physical speed ceiling was established independently of reinforcement learning and bounded to 6\,m/s (\cref{sec:speed-ceiling}).
The bound was established by first principles and validated in simulation.
The bound is a function of the leg workspace, the impulse it can deliver and the mass distribution of the robot.

\subsection{Legality of the gait against the measured envelope}
Legality of the gait against the measured envelope
The audit protocol in one paragraph: replay the greedy runner and check every step against ch3's measured limits (torque, torque-speed, thermal, velocity).
What passes: torque and the torque-speed envelope are respected ~99.9 % of steps.
What fails: swing-leg velocity peaks at ~2× the no-load speed (physically impossible at any gain), and thermal margin is zero — the thigh RMS torque sits at the continuous rating for the entire dash.
Verdict paragraph: the 3.07 m/s figure is therefore optimistic; the missing simulator constraints (velocity limit, winding thermals) are identified and become future-work items, not silent caveats.

\subsection{What the policy actually learned}

The frequency channel is exploited: the policy rails the cadence between its bounds to warp the phase, parking the clock where the spec-rate penalty is cheap and rewriting the gait spec nearly every step.
Distillation test: fitting a single honest Fourier spec to the emitted gait fails (poor fit, left/right asymmetry, coefficient clamping) — the per-step re-parameterisation is load-bearing feedback, not a fixed trajectory being tracked.
Implication paragraph: the generator worked, but as a structured feedback channel rather than an open-loop gait; state what this means for interpreting §4.2 and for deploying the controller.

\subsection{Robustness margins}
Protocol: perturbed greedy evaluations (mass scale, actuation delay, push), not analytical margins — consistent with the definition in §2.1.
The numbers: survival vs mass scaling (the policy has authority margin only below nominal mass), delay margin below one control step; what was and wasn't randomized during the runner's training, stated plainly.
