\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

\subsection{Summary of Findings}

This thesis set out to apply reinforcement learning to a custom high-speed biped, and it delivered
three things. The first is a working method: the modelling, training and curriculum stack that
produces a 2.6\,m/s never-falling runner on the free simulated plant. The second is a measured
robot: every load-bearing parameter of DASH-01 replaced by a measurement, with stated uncertainty
and stated un-resolvables. The third is an honest account of the distance between the two. That
distance is not rhetorical. The measured robot is heavier and weaker than the model it trained
against, and an order of magnitude slower in actuation. Its corrected ceiling of
about 2.7\,m/s burst and 1.3\,m/s sustained reclassifies the platform from record contender to
demonstrator. The learning method itself is vindicated wherever it can be tested. It matches
classical control, and is matched by it, exactly at the plant's own limits, which is what a healthy
learning stack should do.

\subsection{Challenges and Limitations}

The limits of the present work are recorded throughout, and summarised once here. No policy has yet
run on the hardware, and export plus inference on the Pi remain the critical path. The sprint
results rest on privileged observations. Identification left the link inertia tensors and the
0.9\,N$\cdot$m gravity residual unresolved, with the kinematic model as the remaining suspect. The
actuator model lacks a coupled torque--speed envelope, which makes it optimistic exactly at high
speed. The capability analysis's corrected figures are a sensitivity propagation awaiting a full
re-sweep. And the physical robot currently stands with one functional leg.

\subsection{Suggestions for Future Research}
\label{sec:future}

For \emph{this} robot, the record orders four priorities. First, ONNX export and a Pi inference
loop, because everything else waits on them. Second, current-mode control with the position PD
closed on the Pi at 200\,Hz, replacing the 0.8\,Hz drive loop that every measurement identifies as
the binding constraint. Third, the carbon ankle strut. Fourth, the from-scratch cadence retrain.

For a \emph{successor} robot, the measured record ranks the design levers.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Bus voltage before torque.} 87\,\% of feasible gaits are limited at the cam's
        peak-power corner. Speed scales linearly with no-load speed, and not at all with peak
        torque.
  \item \textbf{Distal mass.} At $-0.93$\,(m/s)/kg it carries nine times the leverage of torso
        mass. A carbon shin, plus a proximally mounted spring on a tendon (a true Achilles),
        recovers about 0.5\,m/s for free.
  \item \textbf{Re-proportion or replace the 4-bar.} Only 0.20 to 0.35\,m of a 1.06\,m geometric
        span is usable. The standing pose sits at 96\,\% of reach, where the toe-force ellipse is
        19:1 anisotropic: strong vertically, and weak in exactly the propulsive direction. Target
        a flat transmission ratio over about 0.5\,m, with stance at about 85\,\% of reach.
  \item \textbf{Size the ankle for 3.5 body weights.} The present passive ankle delivers a median
        of 0.42\,BW over the running-stance region, which is 8$\times$ short, and it fails in
        positive mechanical feedback.
  \item \textbf{Cool the cam motor.} Sustained speed is what a 100\,m record is made of, and the
        gap between the 167\,N$\cdot$m demanded and the 55\,N$\cdot$m continuous rating is
        thermal.
  \item \textbf{Treat torque-mode control as an architectural requirement from day one.} That is
        the v1 lesson, and it was paid for twice.
\end{enumerate}

Finally, the validated toe-Jacobian force map and the capability sweep together make a
\emph{design search in simulation} the natural next use of the training cluster. Such a search
would range over bus voltage, gear ratio, distal mass, linkage proportions and ankle stiffness. It
would search over robots, rather than over controllers for a robot already known to be the
constraint.
