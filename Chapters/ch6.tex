\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

This chapter measures the distance between the objective of \cref{sec:intro-objective} and the
results of \cref{sec:results}. It then ranks the changes that would close the gap.

\subsection{Method-level lessons}

Here are the lessons that where learned about the method proposed here.

\textbf{Optimisers exploit objectives}
The policy gamed the reward by skating, penetrating the floor or vibrating on the floor. The
scripted baseline's coordinate-descent tuner gamed its own objective identically.
Thus, objective design should be done with care and tested to find early signs that the objective is being gamed by the optimiser.


\subsection{Future work}

Now that the tooling is in place, the next project should start with a design search rather than a controller as it was originially intended.
This project taught us the correct difficulty and time requirement prior.
We now have hint about mechanical lever that should make the robot much easier to control and that should allow it to run faster.
The first step would be to work on the workspace and it's force map to find a better linkage and ankle stiffness.
The second step would be to implement a feet that is more than a point contact to allow the robot to have a better leverage on the ground and be able to apply a torque and not only a force.
The third step would be to implement and evaluate an impact/force sensor on the foot to allow the robot to have a better feedback on the ground contact and not having to wait for the base to fall over to react.
Another step would be to increase the bus voltage to allow the robot to have a higher no-load speed and therefore be able to run faster if the torque is not saturated at that point.
