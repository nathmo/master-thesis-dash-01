\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

DASH-01 is a 15\,kg biped with six quasi-direct-drive actuators, a parallel 4-bar knee and a
passive ankle. It was built at REHAssist on a student budget, around one question: how fast can a
cheap custom biped run? This thesis takes the platform from a partially finished mechanical design
to a measured, simulated, trained and instrumented machine. It then reports the distance between
what a learned controller achieves in simulation and what the hardware can deliver.

The work covers the whole chain. A CAD-to-MJCF pipeline closes the kinematic loop that URDF cannot
express. A measurement campaign replaces every load-bearing plant parameter with a measured one. A
first-principles feasibility sweep bounds the running speed the mechanism admits, before any
controller exists. Five action-space designs are trained with PPO, under a curriculum that releases
the base degrees of freedom one at a time. A deployment stack closes a 200\,Hz control loop on a
non-realtime Raspberry Pi, with a web bring-up instrument and an always-on flight recorder.

Two measurements carry the argument. Every assumed plant parameter that was later measured moved in
the pessimistic direction. Mass moved by $+18\,\%$ and peak joint torque by $-15\,\%$. The drive's
internal position loop moved by a factor of 16, from an assumed 13\,Hz to a measured 0.8\,Hz,
against a 310\,ms divergence timescale. On the corrected plant the mechanism admits about 2.7\,m/s
in burst and about 1.3\,m/s thermally. The bipedal 100\,m record pace is near 4\,m/s.

The best learned policy runs 60\,s without falling at 2.6\,m/s, on the fully free simulated plant.
A 21-gain scripted controller with no learning in it hits the same pitch-balance wall at the same
1\,s. On this platform the binding constraint is the plant rather than the algorithm. The thesis
therefore ends in a ranked set of mechanical changes rather than in a controller.
